# Ellis Island Inspection Protocol & Legal Standards (1905)

## Legislative & Administrative Framework (circa 1905)

By January 1905, Ellis Island operated under the strict provisions of the **Immigration Act of March 3, 1903**, enforced by the United States Public Health and Marine Hospital Service (PHMHS) and the Bureau of Immigration under Commissioner William Williams (who instituted aggressive anti-corruption reforms between 1902 and 1905).

### Key Legal Grounds for Exclusion in 1905
1. **LPC (Likely to Become a Public Charge):** The most common reason for exclusion. Applied to anyone deemed physically, mentally, or financially incapable of supporting themselves without state charity. Unaccompanied women, pregnant women without husbands present, children traveling without parents, elderly individuals, and those with physical deformities were prime targets.
2. **Contract Labor (Alien Contract Labor Law of 1885):** Immigrants were forbidden from arriving with a pre-arranged job contract. If an immigrant admitted that an American employer, railroad contractor, or *padrone* had guaranteed them work or prepaid their ticket, they were instantly detained for deportation. Yet ironically, they were required to prove they *could* find work to avoid being labeled LPC.
3. **Loathsome or Dangerous Contagious Diseases:** Automatic exclusion without appeal for trachoma, favus, pulmonary tuberculosis, leprosy, or syphilis.
4. **Mental Defects:** Idiots, insane persons, epileptics, and anyone showing symptoms of "feeble-mindedness."
5. **Moral & Political Exclusions:** Convicted felons, polygamists, prostitutes, and anarchists (added under the 1903 Act following President McKinley's assassination in 1901).

## Medical Chalk Mark Shorthand Codes (1905)

Surgeons stood along the railings with sticks of school chalk. In a split second, they marked the right shoulder or lapel of the immigrant's dark coat:

| Code | Medical Suspect / Condition | Fate / Next Step |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **B** | Back (curvature of spine, hunchback, spinal weakness) | Secondary examination in physical clinic |
| **C** | Conjunctivitis (eye inflammation) | Isolated to verify if bacterial or trachoma |
| **Ct** | **Trachoma** (chronic contagious granular conjunctivitis) | **Nearly 50% of all medical exclusions; mandatory deportation** |
| **E** | Eyes (blindness, cataract, ptosis, strabismus) | Detailed ophthalmic examination |
| **F** | Face (abnormal pallor, rashes, facial paralysis) | General clinical exam |
| **Ft** | Feet (clubfoot, deformities, severe flatfoot) | Mobility and labor endurance test |
| **G** | Goiter (thyroid enlargement) | Heart and stamina evaluation |
| **H** | Heart (dyspnea, cyanosis, irregular pulse) | Stethoscope examination in clinic |
| **K** | Hernia / Rupture | Physical strain and manual labor capability check |
| **L** | Lameness (limp, uneven gait, hip dysplasia) | Evaluated for inability to perform manual labor |
| **N** | Neck (swollen cervical lymph nodes, scrofula) | Tuberculosis screening |
| **P** | Physical and lungs (tuberculosis, emaciation) | Sputum test and chest auscultation |
| **Pg** | Pregnancy | Scrutinized for paternity; high risk for LPC if unmarried |
| **Sc** | Scalp (Favus / ringworm of the scalp) | Mandatory exclusion if fungal favus confirmed |
| **S** | Senility (elderly, weak) | High risk for LPC; requires sponsor guarantee |
| **X** | Suspected Mental Defect (confused, timid, erratic) | Held for psychological/puzzle testing |
| **(X)** | Definite Mental Disease / Idiocy | Board of Inquiry; almost certain exclusion |

## The 29 Manifest Questions (Act of 1903)

At the high wooden desks at the end of the iron aisles, immigration inspectors cross-examined each immigrant against the ship's passenger manifest (*List or Manifest of Alien Passengers*):

1. Number on List
2. Full Name
3. Age
4. Sex
5. Married or Single
6. Calling or Occupation
7. Able to Read? Able to Write? *(Literacy test was NOT yet law in 1905; did not pass until 1917, but answers were recorded)*
8. Nationality
9. Race or People *(Italians were legally bifurcated into "Italian, North" and "Italian, South")*
10. Last Permanent Residence (Country and Town)
11. Seaport of Landing in the United States
12. Final Destination (State and City)
13. Whether having a ticket through to such final destination
14. By whom was passage paid? *(Danger zone: If "brother" or "self", accepted; if "agent" or "company", suspected illegal contract labor)*
15. Whether in possession of $30, and if less, how much? *(Immigrants needed at least $10 to $25 in pocket cash to prove solvency)*
16. Whether ever before in the United States; if so, when and where?
17. Whether going to join a relative or friend; state name, complete address, and relationship
18. Whether ever in prison, almshouse, or institution for care of insane, or supported by charity
19. Whether a Polygamist
20. Whether an Anarchist
21. Whether coming by reason of any offer, solicitation, promise, or agreement to perform labor? *(Catch-22: must answer an emphatic "NO")*
22. Condition of Health, Mental and Physical
23. Deformed or Crippled (Nature, length of time, cause)
24. Height, Complexion, Color of Hair and Eyes
25. Marks of Identification (Scars, tattoos, birthmarks)
26. Place of Birth (Country, City, or Town)
*(Plus manifest oath verifications by ship surgeon and master)*

## Dramatic Tension: The Board of Special Inquiry (BSI)
- Immigrants marked with chalk or failing manifest interrogation were led to detention rooms to await a three-member Board of Special Inquiry.
- Interpreters were often overworked, impatient, or spoke Northern Italian dialects that Southern peasants could barely comprehend.
- Family Separation: If a young child was excluded for trachoma, federal law required that one parent return with the child, while the other parent and older children might be forced to choose between staying in America or returning to Italy.
